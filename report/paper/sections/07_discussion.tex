\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================================
The completed factorial experiment does not support a universal advantage for complex-valued networks. Most complex configurations do not exceed the fixed magnitude baseline, while a subset of architectures does. The architecture surface is therefore heterogeneous, which is consistent with the central motivation for an exhaustive factorial design rather than a single real-versus-complex pair.

The largest marginal contrast in the implemented grid is input domain. Because the centered orthonormal FFT is unitary, the image/Fourier difference is not an information-loss contrast. The two representations expose the same prepared complex tensor in different coordinates. The observed difference is therefore more naturally associated with the locality and aggregation biases of the chosen CNN family. Local spatial neighborhoods, pooling windows, covariance statistics, and global aggregation have different meanings after Fourier transformation.

Normalization and stream design provide a second layer of architectural variation. RMS normalization is structurally compatible with global phase rotation because its scale depends only on magnitude power. Complex BatchNorm is more flexible because it whitens real-imaginary covariance and then applies a learned Cartesian affine map. The dual-stream architectures separately expose magnitude and complex features and can fuse them either late or through interaction modules. These factors make it possible to distinguish the retention of complex information from the degree to which the network is constrained to process that information in a phase-compatible way.

The convolution and activation factors sharpen this distinction. Standard complex convolution is strictly complex-linear, whereas widely-linear convolution adds a conjugate pathway and represents a broader real-linear class. modReLU and magnitude-gated SiLU preserve complex direction under global rotation, while CReLU and cardioid do not. The factorial design therefore spans a continuum from stronger algebraic structure to more flexible Cartesian processing. The observed marginal means should be interpreted in that architectural context rather than as isolated rankings of operators.

The canonical pooling family is useful as a tightly controlled subexperiment because only the local aggregation rule changes. Magnitude-max and magnitude-median select an observed complex activation; complex averaging produces a vector sum whose magnitude contains explicit pairwise phase-coherence terms. The canonical rows therefore provide an intuitive link between the algebra in Eq.~\eqref{eq:complex-average-coherence} and an otherwise identical network structure.

Several limitations bound the conclusions. First, every architecture has one training realization at seed 10383, so optimization variability is not quantified in the present paper. Second, the same fixed test cohort is used to describe all 480 complex architectures, so the highest observed configuration is subject to model-selection optimism. Third, test metrics are computed at the slice level even though slices within a patient are correlated. Patient-disjoint splitting prevents leakage across train/validation/test, but it does not convert slice-level metrics into patient-level diagnostic accuracy.

Fourth, the real baseline is magnitude-only. It therefore combines two questions: whether retaining real-imaginary information is useful and whether complex algebra is a useful way to process it. An information-matched real-valued network receiving concatenated real and imaginary channels would separate these mechanisms more cleanly. Fifth, the study uses a prepared four-coil T2-weighted subset and an FFT-derived Fourier arm rather than untouched scanner raw k-space. Finally, the binary target is a slice-level PI-RADS grouping rather than pathology-confirmed cancer status, and a complete clinical prostate MRI assessment uses information beyond a single T2-weighted slice.

These limitations also suggest a natural hierarchy for later validation: repeated training realizations for optimization robustness, patient-cluster uncertainty for the fixed cohort, an information-matched real/imaginary baseline, patient-level aggregation, inclusion of complementary diffusion information, and independent external evaluation. Those extensions are intentionally separated from the present factorial characterization.
