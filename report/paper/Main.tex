\begin{abstract}
Magnetic resonance data are acquired as complex-valued signals, yet diagnostic learning pipelines commonly discard phase and operate on magnitude images. We study slice-level PI-RADS-group classification in a prepared four-coil subset of fastMRI Prostate T2-weighted MRI using one fixed real magnitude CNN and 480 parameter-matched complex configurations. The complex factorial grid spans input domain, pooling, normalization, convolution, activation, and stream/interaction design. All 481 runs use seed 10383 and patient-disjoint partitions. Only \ComplexWins/\ComplexTotal complex configurations exceed the real-baseline AUC of \RealAUC. Image-domain models average \ImageAUC AUC and \ImageAP AP, compared with \FourierAUC AUC and \FourierAP AP for Fourier-domain models. The canonical median model is approximately tied with the real baseline, whereas the maximum AUC of \BestAUC is exploratory because all configurations are evaluated on the same held-out patient cohort. The factorial study therefore characterizes complex-valued benefit as architecture- and representation-dependent rather than universal.
\end{abstract}
